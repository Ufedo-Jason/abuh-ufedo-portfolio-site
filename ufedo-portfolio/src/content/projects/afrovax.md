## The problem behind the prototype

Vaccines are only useful when they reach people in a condition that preserves their effectiveness. In places where electricity is unreliable and journeys to health facilities can be long, maintaining the cold chain is not simply a matter of owning a refrigerator. It is also a problem of portability, power availability, thermal stability and day-to-day usability.

AfroVax began with a question: could a compact, portable system make vaccine storage and transport more dependable in underserved settings?

## From idea to engineering decisions

The project brought together a team working across product development, electronics and practical testing. My role as a co-founder and team lead has involved helping translate the problem into a prototype, coordinating the work and contributing to the engineering decisions that shape the system.

The development process has required us to think beyond the cooling element itself. Insulation, heat transfer, airflow, power conversion, battery autonomy, sensing, control and the way a health worker interacts with the device all influence whether the complete system can do its job.

## What prototyping has taught me

A prototype is an argument made with physical materials. It forces assumptions into the open. A design that appears reasonable on paper can behave differently when ambient temperature, solar availability, power interruptions and repeated opening are introduced.

Testing has therefore been central to the work. Each iteration has helped us identify which parts of the system need more attention and which trade-offs deserve another look. The process has reinforced the importance of measuring the behaviour of the complete system rather than relying on the advertised performance of individual components.

## What comes next

The next phase is about improving reliability, documenting performance under controlled conditions and making the design more practical to manufacture and use. The project is ongoing, so I will update this page as validated results become available and as the team determines what technical information can responsibly be shared.
